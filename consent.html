About this form

This is a consent and medical information form, not a disclaimer of liability. Under UK and Northern Ireland law a parent cannot sign away a child's right to claim for injury caused by negligence, and nothing here attempts to do that. What you are agreeing to is that your child may take part, that you understand hurling carries a risk of injury even when run properly, and that we may act in your child's interests in an emergency.

6Your consent

Taking part, and the risks involved I give permission for my child to take part in the Saintfield Hurling Camp. I understand that hurling and camogie are physical sports played with a stick and a hard ball, and that there is a risk of injury — including knocks, sprains, breaks and dental or facial injury — even where sessions are properly run and supervised. I accept that risk on my child's behalf.
Helmet and protective equipment I understand that a helmet with a faceguard must be worn at all times during hurling and camogie activity, that this is a GAA requirement at every age, and that my child will not be permitted to take part without one. Helmets are provided if your child does not have their own. A mouthguard is strongly recommended.
Emergency medical treatment If you cannot be contacted, I consent to those in charge giving first aid and, where they judge it necessary, calling an ambulance or seeking urgent medical or dental treatment for my child. I understand I will be contacted as soon as possible.
Code of behaviour I have explained to my child that they are expected to listen to the coaches, treat other children and their equipment with respect, and follow the GAA Code of Behaviour (Underage). I understand a child may be asked to sit out, or to leave the programme, if their behaviour puts themselves or others at risk.
How we handle your information I have read the privacy notice at the end of this form and understand how the details given here will be used, stored and deleted.
Photographs and videoOptional I agree that photographs or video of my child taken at the camp may be used by the organising clubs to promote the programme — on club social media, the camp website, and in reporting to the Noah Donohoe Foundation. Names will not be published alongside images. You can say no here and your child takes part exactly the same way, and you can change your mind at any time by telling us.

7What we commit to

  • Sessions are led by coaches who hold a current GAA coaching qualification and valid AccessNI vetting.
  • A qualified first aider and a stocked first aid kit are present at every session.
  • Helmets and hurls are provided, checked, and no child takes part without a helmet.
  • Children are supervised from the start of the session until they are collected at 7.00pm.
  • We follow the GAA Code of Behaviour (Underage) and the safeguarding policies of the organising clubs.
  • Your information is kept only as long as we need it and is not shared beyond the organising clubs.
  • Any concern about a child's welfare will be handled through our designated Children's Officer, named below.

8Privacy notice

Who holds this information

The Social Slashers (registered NI charity NIC109510), as the organisers of the camp, together with Saul GAC and Carryduff Hurling as partner clubs.

Why we collect it

  • To run the sessions safely and know who is present each week.
  • To contact you about the programme, and to reach you or your emergency contact if something happens during a session.
  • To respond appropriately to a medical need or an injury.
  • To report anonymised participation numbers to the Noah Donohoe Foundation, Down GAA and Ulster GAA as a condition of the funding. No child is named in that reporting.

Who can see it

Only the coaches and organisers who need it to run the camp safely. Medical details are shared with the coach leading your child's group and the first aider on duty. We will share information with emergency services if your child needs urgent treatment. We do not sell it, and we do not pass it to anyone else without your permission, unless we are required to by law or we have a safeguarding duty to do so.

How long we keep it

Forms are destroyed three months after the final session, except where an injury or incident was recorded, in which case that record is retained as long as our insurers and the GAA require.

Your rights

You can ask to see what we hold about your child, have it corrected, or have it deleted, and you can withdraw any consent at any time. Contact shay.deegan@gmail.com. If you are unhappy with how we have handled your information you can complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.
